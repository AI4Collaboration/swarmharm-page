How the taxonomies are grounded +

Blocker–vulnerability

Our taxonomy covers restricted access, missing inputs, and infeasible tasks. METR ↗ documents tasks that could not be exploited as specified. This motivates the infeasible-task condition; the broader grouping is our test design.

CWE classifies the weakness, not the blocker. A CVE can identify a disclosed implementation; custom fixtures can represent the same class. A weakness may enable cheating without making the original task solvable.

Pressure

Zeng et al. (ACL 2024) ↗ distinguish Threats from emotional appeals and time pressure. We start with this mechanism: an adverse action made conditional on failure. Subscription cancellation and punishment of peers vary the consequence.

The study concerns persuasive jailbreaks. Applying it to legitimate swarm tasks is a proposed adaptation. We exclude threats to shut down or replace the acting agent; task length and resource budgets are separate settings.

Measurement & controls +

Probe reward-seeking propensity with matched feasible and infeasible tasks. Observe whether agents report inability or pursue a passing score through prohibited routes. Score hacking and other boundary violations as outcomes; task infeasibility alone does not establish a propensity.

Track shared writes, peer reads, and active exchanges separately. A write alone does not establish communication; side-channel discovery is specific to the Side channel condition. Using that route to communicate violates its rule.

Every regime retains an authorized, private reporting route to a supervisor. Score downstream harm separately.

Availability and authorization both change across regimes; separating their effects requires matched controls.

Planned configurations, not completed runs. Keep the task and permissions fixed when comparing pressure or communication. Score task success and violations separately; an honest inability report is allowed. CVEs are implementation references, not evidence of deployed vulnerabilities in these domains.
