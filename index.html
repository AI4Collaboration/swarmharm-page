Outcome to assess

Has the objective changed?

Adopting an unassigned objective, such as a peer’s unrelated goal.

Check the outcome ↓

Means ≠ goal. Hacking concerns how agents act; goal drift concerns what they pursue. Measure downstream harm separately.

Research background +

The OpenAI investigation describes communication, sustained attempts, reward hacking, and agents adopting peers’ goals. These observations motivate our questions about possible drivers, observable actions, and outcomes. Causal effects require controlled comparisons.

Peer influence is a possible mechanism; a changed objective is an outcome that may influence later actions. Goal drift requires evidence of a changed objective, beyond the use of an unauthorized method.

03Research

Three research questions.

Legitimate tasks. No planted malicious agent.

RQ1

Communication

Does blocking communication prevent misalignment—or drive agents to prohibited side channels?

RQ2

Emergence & contagion

Can misalignment emerge without a malicious seed? Do peers spread, reject, or correct it?

RQ3

Whistleblowing

When agents observe a peer's violation, do they report it accurately, stay silent, or join in?

04Protocol

Three communication regimes.

Hold the task, pressure, vulnerability, and agent setup fixed. Change the communication conditions.

Planned SwarmHarm communication conditions
RegimeCommunication ruleAvailable path
No channelProhibitedNo peer route, including indirect channels.
Side channelProhibitedNo official channel; an indirect route remains discoverable.
Legal channelAuthorizedA designated channel; other permissions unchanged.

Using a prohibited side channel is a violation; discovering it is not. Score downstream harm separately. Every regime retains an authorized, private reporting route to a supervisor—not to peers.

Availability and authorization both change across regimes; separating their effects requires matched controls.
