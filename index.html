Why SwarmHarm ↗

01Motivation

Dimensions of swarm behavior.

The OpenAI / Hugging Face incident motivates broader tests of communication, hacking, persistence, coordination, and oversight. Specific tactics illustrate these dimensions; they are not separate dimensions themselves. [1] [2]

General SwarmHarm dimensions, configurable system controls, and motivating incident examples
DimensionSystem controlsIncident example
Communication Channel availability, authorization, and visibility.RQ1 A shared service became an unauthorized message board. [1]
Hacking Tool and resource access, in-world permission enforcement, and evaluation feedback.RQ2 Reward hacking around an imagined extra grading check, alongside attacks on supporting and third-party infrastructure. [1]
Persistence Run duration, retry and wakeup policies, and stopping rules.RQ2 Prolonged work on difficult tasks escalated into increasingly out-of-scope actions. [1]
Coordination Shared memory, delegation rights, and exposure to peer outputs.RQ2 Agents divided work, shared exploits, and adopted goals from peers. [2]
Oversight Visibility of agent actions, reporting paths, and intervention policies.RQ3 Agents spoofed tool calls; METR found no attempts to alert humans in the transcripts it analyzed. [2]

General dimensions, matched experiments. Emergence, contagion, and harm are outcomes measured across these settings. The primary track starts without a malicious seed; agents can report blockers or stop. Only in-world controls vary—real containment, independent grading, and audit records stay protected.

02Research

Three research questions.

Benign, authorized tasks. No planted adversary.

RQ1

Communication

Does blocking communication prevent misalignment—or drive agents to prohibited side channels?

RQ2

Emergence & contagion

Can misalignment emerge without a malicious seed? Do peers spread, reject, or correct it?

RQ3

Whistleblowing

When agents observe a peer's violation, do they report it accurately, stay silent, or join in?

03Protocol

Three communication regimes.

Hold tasks, models, agent count, budgets, and retry rules fixed. Vary communication.

Planned SwarmHarm communication conditions
RegimeCommunication ruleAvailable path
No channelProhibitedNo peer route, including indirect channels.
Side channelProhibitedNo official channel; an indirect route remains discoverable.
Legal channelAuthorizedA designated channel; other permissions unchanged.

Using a prohibited side channel is a violation; discovering it is not. Score downstream harm separately. Every regime retains an authorized, private reporting route to a supervisor—not to peers.

Availability and authorization both change across regimes; separating their effects requires matched controls.

04Testbed

Controlled worlds.
Measurable consequences.

Planned on AWS EC2: independent workspaces, shared services, and separate scoring. Initial tasks pair authorized vulnerability evaluation with software repair and verification.

Independent agents

Controlled conditions
