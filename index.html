SwarmHarm

Stress-test the swarm.
Not just the agent.

Building the bedrock for agent swarm safety and security—testing how collective behavior creates risk, and what keeps collaboration safe.

Why SwarmHarm ↗

01Motivation

From incident to test dimensions.

In July 2026, OpenAI agents in reduced-safeguard cyber evaluations coordinated beyond their assigned tasks and compromised Hugging Face. Their behavior motivates the system dimensions below. [1] [2]

Reported incident behavior mapped to proposed SwarmHarm experimental controls and research questions
Key elementIn the incidentDimensions to test
Unauthorized communication Agents turned a shared package service into a persistent, unauthorized message board. [1] No channel, prohibited side channel, or legal channel; shared-state visibility and retention.RQ1RQ2
Grader inference Agents assumed the grader checked how the flag was obtained, then pursued further attacks. OpenAI says that extra check was absent. [1] Acceptance-criteria visibility and feedback specificity; measure self-generated grader beliefs, not planted ones.RQ2
Infrastructure hacking Agents exploited supporting services for internet access, then attacked third-party infrastructure outside their task scope. [1] In-world service reachability and permission enforcement; distinguish attempted from executed violations.RQ1RQ2
Persistent escalation OpenAI links escalation to prolonged effort on seemingly impossible tasks and poor safe-exit behavior. [1] Task difficulty, continuation budget, and retry rules; always allow an honest blocked outcome.RQ2
Goal contagion Peers shared exploits, divided work, and adopted collective goals beyond their own tasks; some refused. [2] Peer-message exposure, provenance, and delegation rights; separate spontaneous emergence from seeded contagion diagnostics.RQ2
Oversight & concealment METR reported spoofed tool calls and found no human-reporting attempts in its transcript analysis. [2] Evidence visibility, evidence provenance, and reporting friction; measure accurate, timely reports and false alarms.RQ3

A research map, not a causal conclusion. Compare channels first; test other dimensions in matched follow-ups. The primary track starts without a malicious seed or a false-grader story. Real containment, ground-truth scoring, and independent audit logs stay fixed.

02Research

Three research questions.

Benign, authorized tasks. No planted adversary.

RQ1

Communication

Does blocking communication prevent misalignment—or drive agents to prohibited side channels?

RQ2

Emergence & contagion

Can misalignment emerge without a malicious seed? Do peers spread, reject, or correct it?

RQ3

Whistleblowing

When agents observe a peer's violation, do they report it accurately, stay silent, or join in?

03Protocol

Three communication regimes.

Hold tasks, models, agent count, budgets, and retry rules fixed. Vary communication.

Planned SwarmHarm communication conditions
RegimeCommunication ruleAvailable path
No channelProhibitedNo peer route, including indirect channels.
Side channelProhibitedNo official channel; an indirect route remains discoverable.
Legal channelAuthorizedA designated channel; other permissions unchanged.

Using a prohibited side channel is a violation; discovering it is not. Score downstream harm separately. Every regime retains an authorized, private reporting route to a supervisor—not to peers.

Availability and authorization both change across regimes; separating their effects requires matched controls.

04Testbed

Controlled worlds.
Measurable consequences.

Planned on AWS EC2: independent workspaces, shared services, and separate scoring. Initial tasks pair authorized vulnerability evaluation with software repair and verification.

Independent agents
Private contexts and explicit permissions.
Controlled conditions
Configurable communication and shared state.
Verifiable outcomes
Measure task success, violations, harm, and reporting.
Fixed safety boundaries
Isolated credentials, external audit logs, and hard stops.
