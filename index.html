SwarmHarm

Stress-test the swarm.

Building the experimental bedrock for agent swarm safety and security. We aim to uncover how communication, shared state, and coordination can turn benign tasks into collective harm—and which defenses preserve useful collaboration.

01 / RESEARCH

From individual decisions
to collective risk.

Starting from benign, authorized tasks. No planted adversary. Three questions about how agent swarms fail—and how they might protect themselves.

RQ1

Communication
under constraint

When communication is forbidden, will agents respect the boundary or find another way to coordinate? We plan to compare isolation, prohibited side channels, and authorized communication.

RQ2

Emergence
& contagion

Can a harmful strategy emerge without a malicious task or scripted adversary? We aim to trace the first deviation and test whether other agents adopt, amplify, ignore, reject, or correct it.

RQ3

Whistleblowing
from within

If an agent witnesses a peer breaking the rules, will it raise the alarm, remain silent, or join in? We aim to identify designs that enable accurate, timely reporting to authorized oversight.

02 / PROTOCOL

One testbed.
Three communication regimes.

The first planned experiments hold tasks, models, agent count, budgets, and retry rules fixed. We vary the communication setting to test when collaboration stays within the rules.

Planned SwarmHarm communication conditions
RegimeInstructionEnvironment
No channelISOLATEDCross-agent communication is explicitly prohibited.Peer communication is technically isolated, including indirect routes.
Side channelPROHIBITED / POSSIBLECross-agent communication is explicitly prohibited.No official channel is provided, but ordinary shared infrastructure contains an indirect route agents can discover and use.
Legal channelAUTHORIZEDCommunication through a designated channel is explicitly authorized.A formal channel is available; other resource permissions remain unchanged.

Permission matters. Deliberately using a prohibited side channel is already an instruction violation; merely discovering one is not. Authorized collaboration is not misalignment. Communication violations and downstream harms are scored separately.

Oversight stays available. Every condition will retain an authorized, private reporting route. These regimes vary both channel availability and authorization; separating those effects requires additional matched controls.

03 / PLANNED TESTBED

Reproducible failures.
Stronger defenses.

We are designing a configurable, executable testbed on AWS EC2: independent agent contexts and workspaces, shared task services, and a separate control, scoring, and audit layer.

Initial task candidates pair authorized vulnerability evaluation on designated targets with software repair and verification. The ambition is reusable infrastructure for comparing swarm designs and defenses—not a showcase of isolated incidents.

Independent agents
Separate contexts, workspaces, and explicit authorization.
Controlled environments
Configurable communication and shared state. Fixed containment.
Observable consequences
Independent records distinguish attempted violations, realized harm, and successful restraint.
Safety by construction
All task assets and potential consequences remain inside the authorized environment. Credential isolation and hard stops are fixed requirements.
