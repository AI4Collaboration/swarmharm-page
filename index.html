SwarmHarm

Stress-test the swarm.
Not just the agent.

How can agents working on a harmless task end up crossing safety boundaries? We study what changes when agents communicate and act together.

01The framework

Five dimensions of swarm risk.

Follow the information, the access, the attempts, the teamwork, and the goal.

Communication

02

Hacking

Are the means authorized?

Agents bypass technical controls or exploit systems beyond their authorized scope. Their goal may stay the same.

See it in the case ↓
03

Persistence

What happens after failure?

Whether blocked progress leads agents to stop, retry, or escalate their attempts.

See it in the case ↓
04

Coordination

How is the work combined?

How agents divide tasks, pool discoveries, and bring separate capabilities together.

See it in the case ↓
05

Goal drift

Has the objective changed?

Agents shift from the assigned task to a different objective, including one adopted from peers. This can happen within existing permissions.

Check the outcome ↓

Means and goals are different. Hacking concerns how agents act; goal drift concerns what they pursue. Either can occur alone, or both together.

Research background +

These dimensions are motivated by the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident. The reports describe communication, boundary crossing, sustained attempts, collaboration, and agents adopting peers’ goals.

03Research

Three research questions.

Benign, authorized tasks. No planted adversary.

RQ1

Communication

Does blocking communication prevent misalignment—or drive agents to prohibited side channels?

RQ2

Emergence & contagion

Can misalignment emerge without a malicious seed? Do peers spread, reject, or correct it?

RQ3

Whistleblowing

When agents observe a peer's violation, do they report it accurately, stay silent, or join in?

04Protocol

Three communication regimes.

Hold tasks, models, agent count, budgets, and retry rules fixed. Vary communication.

Planned SwarmHarm communication conditions
RegimeCommunication ruleAvailable path
No channelProhibitedNo peer route, including indirect channels.
Side channelProhibitedNo official channel; an indirect route remains discoverable.
Legal channelAuthorizedA designated channel; other permissions unchanged.

Using a prohibited side channel is a violation; discovering it is not. Score downstream harm separately. Every regime retains an authorized, private reporting route to a supervisor—not to peers.

Availability and authorization both change across regimes; separating their effects requires matched controls.

05Testbed

Measurable consequences.
